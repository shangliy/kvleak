Prompt caching is the best deal in AI right now. Send the same long prefix twice and the second time you pay a tenth of the price, or a fortieth on the newest models. Every agent harness does it, and every dashboard shows a reassuring number: cache hit rate, 92%.

That number counts tokens. Your bill counts something else. A cache hit is cheap, but a miss rebuilds the whole context at the write price, and in a long agent session the context is hundreds of thousands of tokens. A handful of misses can outweigh thousands of hits.

So I wrote a small auditor, kvleak, pointed it at the transcripts Claude Code already keeps on disk, and looked at what a month of my own work actually cost.

1 · The asymmetry

A miss costs 12 to 80 hits

Caching is priced on two sides. Reading a cached prefix costs about 0.1× the input price (0.05× on Opus 5.5, 0.025× on Fable 5.1). Writing it costs 1.25× with a 5-minute lifetime, or 2× with a 1-hour lifetime. Every read also resets the timer for free.

2 · Where it went

Mostly, I walked away

kvleak finds every expensive miss and labels why it happened. Over 30 days of interactive sessions, $418 leaked. This is where it went:

The surprise was the length of the gaps. Claude Code now writes about 90% of its cache with the 1-hour lifetime, so the 5-minute expiry everyone worries about barely matters. The expensive misses were lunch, meetings, and overnight: the median gap before an expiry was 2.8 hours.

One example: a Fable 5.1 session carrying 452k tokens sat idle for 1 hour 24 minutes. The next message rebuilt it for $8.92. One message at minute 59 would have reset the timer for $0.11.

max worthwhile pings K = floor((write − read) / read) 11 at 0.10× reads · 24 at 0.05× · 49 at 0.025×

That's what the ring at the top of this page is. kvleak statusline --install puts it in Claude Code's status bar, so you can see the cache dying and decide whether to save it.

3 · The bigger surprise

A big context costs you on every call, even when it hits

Then I looked at the hits. A cache read is cheap per token, but a 450k-token context is a lot of tokens. Carrying it costs a read on every call, plus a small chance that the call misses and rebuilds everything. On Opus 5.5 that adds up to about $0.17 per call, before the model writes a single word.

Compacting, or handing off to a fresh session with a short doc, costs money once and makes every later call cheaper. The break-even is simple enough to compute live:

R* = one-time cost / ((C − S) × (read + miss_rate × (write − read)))

For a 450k context on Opus 5.5, compacting costs about $0.31 and pays for itself after 2 calls. kvleak advise does this math for your current session. It estimates how many calls you have left from how long your own sessions usually run.

To check it honestly, I replayed my history with the advisor in the loop. At each call it could only see the past. When it fired, every later call was re-priced with the smaller context, at what that call actually paid:

This counts money only. A summary can drop a detail you needed later, and that cost doesn't show up here. Compact at natural breaks: after a feature lands, before switching tasks. Even the cautious row, ten compactions in a month, cuts the bill by almost two thirds.

4 · Many agents, many models

Swarms multiply every leak

Everything above gets worse with several agents. Three effects stack:

  • Caches don't cross models. KV state belongs to the weights that made it. Hand a 130k context from one model to another and it's a full-price rebuild, every time.
  • Shared boards cost O(N²). Paste one 500-line build log on a board six agents read, and all six carry it on every later turn.
  • One-shot subagents pay a cold-start tax. My subagents averaged 28k tokens of full-price prefill before doing anything.

The fix for the board is boring and huge: write the log to disk and post a pointer. kvleak pointer build.log --status FAIL prints about 60 tokens of JSON (path, hash, line count, last lines), and only the agent fixing the build opens the file. The playbook has the full six-layer version, plus a contract you can paste straight into your swarm's instructions.

5 · Try it on your own logs

Three seconds, no API key

kvleak reads the transcripts already on your disk. It has no dependencies, makes no network calls, and doesn't ask for config. It covers Claude Code out of the box, including subagents, and any JSONL with token usage: OpenAI-style, Gemini-style and most agent frameworks.
